01 / About

From genes to
field-scale patterns

I am a Plant Science Researcher (Research Scientist III) in the Schnable Lab at Iowa State University. My work sits at the intersection of quantitative genetics, plant biology, high-throughput phenotyping, and predictive modeling.

My research asks how maize plants coordinate canopy and root development under crowding and shade. By connecting genes with 3D architecture and field performance, I aim to help create crops that capture light more efficiently and remain productive under environmental stress.

02 / Research

How do plants thrive
when space and light are limited?

My program links molecular mechanisms to canopy-scale outcomes across three connected research directions.

A

Plant-light signaling

Discovering how shade signals are perceived and transmitted among leaves, stalks, and roots — with a focus on auxin, photoreceptors, and density-dependent development.

Molecular genetics
B

Canopy architecture & phenotyping

Building image-based, UAV, and time-series phenotyping systems to measure leaf orientation, light interception, and plant architecture from individual organs to entire fields.

Phenomics
C

Predictive crop design

Combining multi-omic data, quantitative genetics, and 3D modeling to predict crop performance and identify architectures suited to dense planting and changing environments.

Modeling

A central finding

Maize canopies can self-organize to capture more light.

My colleagues and I showed that maize leaves can reorient perpendicular to planting rows in response to neighboring plants. This density-dependent behavior reduces overlap, improves light interception, and is genetically regulated as part of the shade-avoidance response.
